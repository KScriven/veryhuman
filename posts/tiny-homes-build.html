Building Tiny Homes

23 October 2026

Small Spaces, Big Ideas: My First Tiny House Build

From writing code to working with timber. An introduction to making things that don't require a Wi-Fi connection.

What I tried

I've spent much of my working life building things with code. Things that live on screens, run on servers and occasionally break for reasons that make absolutely no sense. Recently, I've found myself wanting to build something a little more tangible. Something I can touch. Something that requires tools rather than a keyboard. Something that might even still be standing when the Wi-Fi goes down. And so began my introduction to tiny house building. I was curious about more than just how to construct a small building. I wanted to understand what goes into creating a space that's functional, thoughtfully designed and built with your own hands. Armed with enthusiasm and very little practical building experience, I signed up for a course.

What I noticed

There's something both exciting and uncomfortable about being a complete beginner again. In software engineering, I've developed the experience to recognise patterns, solve problems and generally know what I'm doing. Put a piece of timber in front of me, however, and suddenly I'm asking very different questions. Which way does this go? How do I measure that? And why does everyone else seem to understand the drawing? What surprised me was how much thinking happens before anything gets built. It's not simply a matter of cutting wood and joining it together. There's a whole sequence of decisions, measurements and dependencies that need to make sense. Actually, perhaps it's not so different from software engineering after all.

What I'm learning

I'm beginning to appreciate that building well requires more than knowing how to use tools. It requires patience, accuracy and an understanding of how individual pieces fit into something bigger. I'm also discovering that I enjoy the physical side of learning. Working with materials, seeing progress take shape and having something tangible at the end of the day. There's something satisfying about stepping away from a screen and engaging a completely different part of my brain. And unlike software, you can't always undo a mistake with a keyboard shortcut.

Next time

I want to start putting these new skills into practice independently. Smaller projects, more time handling tools and plenty of opportunities to get things wrong without bringing an entire structure down. I'm particularly interested in developing my woodworking and joinery skills, understanding construction drawings and becoming more confident with measurements. I'm not entirely sure where all of this will lead. But I'm enjoying being curious, being a beginner and finding out what happens when I swap a keyboard for a toolbox.
Sometimes the most interesting thing you can build is a new direction for yourself.
